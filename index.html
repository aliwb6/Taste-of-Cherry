02 / The director

Abbas
Kiarostami

Filmmaker, photographer, poet.

Born in Tehran in 1940, Kiarostami became a defining figure of the Iranian New Wave. His films make ordinary encounters feel open-ended: part story, part observation, part invitation to think.

19402016

03 / The story

One drive.
Three encounters.

Mr. Badii travels through the outskirts of Tehran looking for someone willing to help with a private, grave request. Along the way, strangers respond from different beliefs, needs, and experiences.

The film withholds easy explanations—and that restraint matters.

04 / Visual storytelling

Space does
the speaking.

LandscapesDry hills and roads turn distance into a feeling.
Long takesTime is allowed to stretch, like a real conversation.
Minimal dialogueWhat remains unsaid becomes part of the film.

05 / Deeper meanings

It asks us
to notice.

Loneliness can make the world feel very small.

Connection can arrive in an unexpected conversation.

Life may be held together by attention to ordinary things.

The film never forces a conclusion.

06 / International recognition

Palme d’Or

Cannes
1997

Taste of Cherry became the first Iranian film to win the Cannes Film Festival’s top prize. Its recognition helped bring wider international attention to Iranian cinema.

Shared that year with The Eel by Shohei Imamura

07 / My interpretation

A film that
trusts me.

I find this film powerful because it does not tell me what to feel. It gives me time to sit with difficult questions, and it reminds me that listening can be an act of care.

Quiet cinema can be deeply active.

08 / Closing
